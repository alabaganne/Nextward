Remote jobs you can do from Tunisia exist, but many listings marked "remote" only accept people in certain countries. Look for roles that name Tunisia, Africa, a wide region or "anywhere", confirm it with the employer, then check the contract type and how you will be paid before you accept. Tax, social security and foreign currency rules apply to you as a resident of Tunisia, so check them with an accountant or the official sources.

## Where can I find remote jobs from Tunisia?

- **Nextward.** The [remote from Tunisia](/opportunities/remote-from-tunisia) page lists roles where the source shows that people in Tunisia can apply. You can save your search and open the original application page from each listing.
- **Career pages of remote-first companies.** Many fully remote companies publish the list of countries where they can hire. Check that list before anything else.
- **Remote job boards.** They collect remote roles from many companies. Always filter or read by location, because many roles are limited to the US or Europe.
- **LinkedIn.** Search for your role with the "remote" filter, then read each listing's location rules. Follow companies that hire across regions.
- **Freelance marketplaces.** Useful for contract work and short missions. Check fees, payment methods and payout options for Tunisia before you start.
- **Your network.** Former colleagues and classmates abroad, open-source projects and online communities often hear about roles first.
- **Tunisian companies with foreign clients.** Many offer remote or hybrid work with a local contract. This is often the simplest setup for taxes and pay.

## How do I know if a remote job accepts people in Tunisia?

Read the location rules, not just the "remote" label. Our guide on how to [check remote job eligibility from Tunisia](/blog/check-remote-job-eligibility-from-tunisia) explains this in detail. In short:

### Good signs

- Tunisia is named in the list of countries.
- "Africa", "North Africa" or "EMEA" is named, and the company confirms that Tunisia is included.
- "Work from anywhere", with no country list that excludes Tunisia.

### Warning signs

- "Remote (US)", "Remote, EU only" or a single country in brackets.
- "Must be authorised to work in…" followed by a country where you have no right to work.
- Benefits or payroll described only for one or two countries.

If it is unclear, send a short question before you write a long application: "I'm based in Tunisia (UTC+1). Is this role open to candidates there, and how would the contract work?"

## What time zone does Tunisia use for remote work?

Tunisia stays on UTC+1 all year, with no daylight saving time. So:

- In winter, you are on the same time as Paris and Berlin.
- In summer, when Europe moves its clocks, you are one hour behind them.
- You are 5 to 6 hours ahead of the US East Coast, depending on the season.

This makes European teams easy to work with. For US teams, expect some meetings in your late afternoon or evening. Say clearly in your application which hours you can overlap.

## What contract types are there for remote work?

How you are hired changes your rights, your taxes and how you get paid. Here are the main setups:

| Setup | What it means | What to check |
|---|---|---|
| Employee through an employer of record (EOR) | A third-party company becomes your legal employer and hires you on behalf of the foreign company, which has no legal presence in Tunisia | Which company is your legal employer, whether it really covers Tunisia, paid leave, social security, and how the salary reaches you |
| Contractor or freelancer | You are independent. You send invoices and the company pays you for your work | Payment terms, currency, notice period, who owns your work, and the taxes and social security you must handle yourself |
| Employee of a local entity | The company, or a partner, has a legal entity in Tunisia and hires you under a Tunisian contract | Same checks as any local job: contract type, trial period, salary, social security |
| Employee of a Tunisian company with foreign clients | You work remotely, but your employer is in Tunisia | Remote rules, equipment, working hours and travel |

Ask the company which setup it plans to use before the last interview. A good offer on paper can look very different once you know who pays you, and how.

## How do I get paid from abroad?

There are three common ways:

- **Through local payroll,** if an EOR or a local entity employs you. You are paid in Tunisia like any employee.
- **By international bank transfer** to your Tunisian bank account, usually for contractors.
- **Through a payment platform,** often used by freelance marketplaces.

Tunisia has foreign exchange rules, set by law and applied by the Central Bank of Tunisia (BCT) and the banks. Freelancers and small exporters of digital services have publicly reported problems receiving foreign payments. For example, the national association of small and medium businesses (ANPME) asked the BCT in October 2025 to simplify these procedures. In 2026, a bill on online freelance work and electronic payments was filed in parliament. It was still a proposal, not law, when this guide was written. Rules may change, so check what applies today.

Before your first payment, check:

1. **With your bank:** which accounts you can open, which documents it asks for incoming transfers (contract, invoices), the fees and the exchange rate.
2. **With the payment service:** whether it supports receiving money in Tunisia, and how you can withdraw it.
3. **With an accountant:** which status fits your case. Options can include the auto-entrepreneur regime created in 2020, which has conditions such as an annual turnover limit, an individual business, or a company.
4. **In the official sources:** the BCT website for exchange rules and the tax administration for your obligations.

This section is general information, not legal, tax or financial advice. Never use another person's identity or account to receive payments. Accounts can be blocked, and you take on serious risks.

## How do I stand out for remote roles?

Remote teams hire people who work well without someone next to them. Show it:

- **Write clearly.** Your messages, your CV and your cover note are the first test.
- **Show async habits.** Mention written updates, documentation, and tools you have used to work across time zones.
- **State your setup.** Your time zone, your working hours overlap, a quiet workspace and a backup internet connection.
- **Show proof of work.** A portfolio, public code, case studies or results with numbers.
- **Prepare references** from managers or clients who can confirm you deliver on your own.

When you get several offers, compare the full picture: pay after fees and taxes, contract type, benefits, hours and growth. ## How do I avoid remote job scams?

Be careful if a "recruiter":

- asks you to pay for training, equipment or a "processing fee";
- asks for your bank login, card details or ID copies before any real interview;
- runs the whole process by chat, with no video call and no company email;
- sends you a cheque or money and asks you to send part of it back;
- offers pay far above the market for very little work.

Check that the company's website, email domain and LinkedIn page match. Apply through the original application page, which every Nextward listing links to, rather than through a link someone sent you.
