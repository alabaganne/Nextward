To find your first job after graduation in Tunisia, pick one or two target roles, build a CV around your internships and projects, and apply every week on job boards, company pages and LinkedIn. Register with ANETI early so you can use public programmes like SIVP if they fit you, and read every contract before you sign. Expect the search to take time, so treat it as a weekly routine, not a one-time push.

## How hard is it to find a first job as a graduate in Tunisia?

It is a real challenge. According to the National Institute of Statistics (INS), the unemployment rate for higher-education graduates was 26.6% in the second quarter of 2026. The national rate was 14.9% over the same period.

This does not mean you will wait for years. It means a method helps: clear targets, a good CV, steady applications and a network. The rest of this guide shows how.

## What should I do in the first weeks after graduation?

1. **Pick one or two target roles.** "Junior data analyst" or "junior accountant" is a target. "Anything in my field" is not. A clear target makes your CV and your search much sharper.
2. **Update your CV and LinkedIn.** Put your PFE, internships and best projects first, with results. Use the job titles you are aiming for.
3. **Get your documents ready.** Diploma or certificate of success, transcripts, internship certificates, and one or two people who can give a reference.
4. **Register with ANETI.** ANETI is the national employment agency. Registering lets you use its services and public programmes. It now has an online registration platform, which was being rolled out to its employment offices in 2025. If it does not work for your area, go to your local employment office.
5. **Set up your search.** Save searches for [entry-level jobs](/opportunities/jobs?seniority=entry) on Nextward so you can check new offers quickly, and open the original application page from each listing.

## Where can I find entry-level jobs in Tunisia?

- **Job boards and Nextward.** Use filters for entry level, location and work type to cut the noise.
- **Company career pages.** Make a list of 20 to 30 companies you would like to work for and check their pages every week.
- **LinkedIn.** Set job alerts, follow companies, and read posts from recruiters and team leads.
- **Your PFE and internship companies.** Ask your supervisor if there is an opening, or if they know a team that is hiring.
- **Your school.** Career offices, job fairs and alumni groups often share offers with recent graduates.
- **Remote roles.** Some foreign companies hire from Tunisia. Our guide to [remote jobs from Tunisia](/blog/remote-jobs-from-tunisia) explains how to check eligibility.

Talk to people too. A short message to a former classmate who now works at a company you like can lead to a referral. A referral can help your CV get read.

## Is SIVP a good option for a first job?

SIVP is a public programme for first-time job seekers who hold a higher-education diploma, managed through ANETI. In recent ANETI texts it is called CIVP. Its rules have changed over time, so we keep the details in a separate guide: read our article on the [SIVP contract in Tunisia](/blog/sivp-contract-tunisia) and check the current rules with ANETI.

Before you accept one, ask yourself three questions:

- Is there a real job at the end, or only the programme period?
- Will you learn skills that help your next step?
- Are the tasks close to the role you are aiming for?

If the answers are mostly yes, it can be a good first step.

## What contract will I get for my first job?

Tunisia changed its labour rules in 2025. Law n° 2025-9, published in the Official Journal (JORT) on 23 May 2025, makes the permanent contract (CDI) the normal rule. Fixed-term contracts (CDD) are now limited to exceptional cases. The trial period is set at six months, renewable only once.

Before you sign, check:

- The type of contract: CDI, CDD, or a programme like SIVP.
- The length of the trial period, and whether it can be renewed.
- Your job title, tasks, place of work and working hours.
- Your gross and net salary, and when you are paid.
- Your social security (CNSS) declaration.
- Notice periods and any clause that limits other work.

This is general information, not legal advice. For your own situation, read the official text in the Official Journal (JORT) or ask the labour inspection or a lawyer.

## How do I talk about salary for my first job?

- **Do your research first.** Ask alumni and friends in similar roles what juniors earn. Look at offers that show a salary range.
- **Ask whether figures are gross or net.** In Tunisia, people often talk about both (brut and net). Make sure you compare the same thing.
- **Look at the full package.** Transport, meals, health insurance, training, remote days and the chance to grow all have value.
- **Give a range, not a single number,** if the recruiter asks for your expectations.
- **Compare offers side by side.** Our guide to [comparing job opportunities](/blog/compare-job-opportunities-as-a-professional) gives a simple method.

## What if I don't find a job after a few months?

Look at where things stop:

- **Few or no interviews?** The problem is likely your CV or your targets. Rewrite your CV for each type of role, and ask someone in the field to review it.
- **Interviews but no offers?** Work on your interview answers. Our guide to [interview questions](/blog/internship-interview-questions) is written for interns, but the same methods work for junior roles.
- **No offers in your field?** Widen your search to nearby roles, other cities, or remote work.

Meanwhile, keep building: a short course, a personal project, volunteering, or a small freelance task. It gives you something new to talk about in each interview.

## What does a 30-day job search plan look like?

| Week | Focus | By the end of the week |
|---|---|---|
| 1 | Targets and documents | One or two target roles, an updated CV and LinkedIn, ANETI registration started |
| 2 | Lists and searches | 20 to 30 target companies, saved searches, first 10 applications |
| 3 | Network | 10 short messages to alumni, former supervisors and classmates |
| 4 | Review and adjust | Count applications, answers and interviews, then fix the weakest step |

Repeat weeks 2 to 4 until you sign. Keep a simple table of every application, its date and its answer.
