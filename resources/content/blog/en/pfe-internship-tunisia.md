A PFE internship (projet de fin d'études) is the final-year project that many Tunisian licence, master and engineering programs require before you graduate. To land one, start searching in the autumn before your start date, apply to topics that match your specialty with a one-page CV and a short letter, follow up once or twice, and check the topic, supervision and paperwork before you accept.

## What is a PFE internship?

The PFE is a real project you carry out in a company or a lab, usually during your last semester. It ends with a written report (mémoire) and a defense in front of a jury. For many students, it is also the first step into a job.

The rules depend on your school. For example, ENIT's student guide plans a third-year semester with at least 16 weeks for the PFE, supervised by at least one teacher. Other schools and programs set other lengths and dates, so read your own school's rules first.

Three parties are involved in your PFE:

- **You**, the student.
- **Your school**, through an academic supervisor (encadrant universitaire) and the internship office.
- **The host company**, through a company supervisor (encadrant professionnel).

Their roles are written in an internship agreement (convention de stage) that everyone signs before you start.

## When should you start looking for a PFE?

Start early. Large companies often publish a list of PFE topics, sometimes called a « PFE book », months before the internships begin. Tunisie Telecom, for example, presented its 2023–2024 PFE book in early December 2023.

Here is a simple plan for a PFE that starts in February. Shift it to match your own dates.

| When | What to do |
|---|---|
| September–October | Pick 2–3 target fields, update your CV, list about 30 companies |
| October–December | Apply to PFE books, posted offers and a few companies directly |
| December–January | Pass tests and interviews, follow up, compare offers |
| 2–3 weeks before the start | Get your school's approval and sign the agreement |

If your school runs the PFE at another time of year, keep the same gaps between steps.

## Where can you find PFE offers in Tunisia?

Use several channels at once. Each one reaches different companies.

- **Company PFE books and career pages.** Banks, telecom operators, consulting firms and software companies often publish topics with a deadline.
- **Your school's internship office.** Companies send topics straight to schools. Teachers also hear about projects and lab topics.
- **Job and internship sites.** On Nextward, you can browse [PFE internships](/opportunities/internships?program_type=pfe), filter by field and city, save a search, and open the original application page.
- **LinkedIn.** Follow the companies you like and the recruiters who post PFE topics.
- **Alumni and former interns.** Ask students from the year above where they did their PFE and how they got it.
- **Spontaneous applications.** Many small and mid-size companies post nothing but still take PFE students.

## How do you apply for a PFE internship?

Treat each application like a small project. Ten careful applications beat fifty copies of the same email.

1. **Pick a topic, not just a company.** Check that it fits your specialty and what your school expects.
2. **Adapt your CV.** Put the projects closest to the topic at the top. Our guide on [writing an internship CV](/blog/internship-cv) shows how.
3. **Write a short letter or email.** Name the topic, give your dates, and cite one project that proves you can do the work. Our [PFE cover letter guide](/blog/internship-cover-letter) has a structure and a full example.
4. **Send it to the right place.** Use the form or address in the offer. For a spontaneous application, find the HR contact or the team lead.
5. **Track everything.** A simple sheet works: company, topic, date sent, contact, status.

### Show proof of your skills

Recruiters see many CVs that look alike. A GitHub link, a portfolio, a short demo video or a past project report helps them judge your level. If you did a [summer internship](/blog/summer-internship-tunisia), describe one concrete result from it.

### Get ready for tests and interviews

Many PFE processes include a technical test, a case study or a short interview with your future supervisor. Prepare a two-minute introduction and be ready to explain your projects in detail. Practice with our list of [internship interview questions](/blog/internship-interview-questions).

## How should you follow up after applying?

Silence is common. A polite follow-up often gets a reply.

- Wait about one week after the deadline, or after sending a spontaneous application.
- Send a short email: your name, the topic, the date you applied, and one line on why you are still interested.
- If there is no answer, follow up once more the next week. Then put your energy into other offers.
- After an interview, send a thank-you email the same day or the next.

If you get an offer while you wait for another company, ask for a few days to answer. Be honest about the reason.

## What should you check in a PFE offer before you accept?

A good PFE offer is clear and written down. Check these points before you say yes:

- **The topic.** Is it specific and big enough for a full report?
- **Supervision.** Who is your company supervisor, and how often will you meet?
- **School approval.** Your academic supervisor must accept the topic. Ask before you sign.
- **Dates and length.** They must fit your school's calendar and minimum length.
- **Location.** On site, hybrid or remote, and the exact address.
- **Allowance.** Ask if there is one and get the amount in writing. ENIT's agreement template, for example, has a line for a monthly « indemnité de stage ».
- **Equipment and access.** Laptop, software licences, data and the tools you will need.
- **Confidentiality.** What you are allowed to show in your report and at your defense.
- **What comes next.** Does the company hire PFE students after they graduate?

Get the main points confirmed in writing, then sign the internship agreement with your school and the company.

## What comes after the PFE?

Plan the report from the first weeks. Take notes every week, keep screenshots and results, and agree on an outline with both supervisors. Our guide to the [internship report and PFE defense](/blog/internship-report-pfe-defense) helps you get organized.

After graduation, some companies offer an [SIVP contract](/blog/sivp-contract-tunisia), now called CIVP. It is for graduates looking for their first job, not for students. Learn how it works before you discuss it with a recruiter.
