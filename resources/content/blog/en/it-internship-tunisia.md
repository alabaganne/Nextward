IT internships in Tunisia come from many kinds of employers: software service companies, startups, banks and telecom operators, consulting firms, and foreign companies with teams in the country. You find them on company career pages, job boards and LinkedIn, through your school's partner companies, and through clubs and hackathons. A clean GitHub profile with two or three real projects often helps more than a long list of skills on your CV.

## What kinds of companies offer IT internships in Tunisia?

Think wider than the few big names everyone applies to. Interns are needed in many places:

- **Software service and IT consulting companies.** They build software for clients, often in Europe. Many of them take interns, especially for the PFE.
- **Startups.** Small teams, more responsibility, and you often touch many parts of the product. The government's Startup Tunisia site has a [database of labelled startups](https://startup.gov.tn/en/database) with their sector and website, which is a good list to explore.
- **Banks, insurers, telecom operators and large groups.** They have internal IT, data and security teams.
- **Embedded and engineering companies.** Electronics, automotive software and industrial systems.
- **Digital agencies.** Websites, mobile apps, e-commerce and marketing tools.
- **Public bodies and research labs.** Often through your school or your teachers.

Each type gives a different experience. A startup teaches you speed and range. A large company teaches you process, code review and working at scale.

## Where are the tech parks in Tunisia?

Several technology parks group many tech companies in one place. They are useful for building a list of companies to check:

- **El Ghazala technology park (Ariana, Greater Tunis).** Created in 1999 and focused on communication technologies. It hosts many software and telecom companies.
- **Sfax technology park.** It has a business park for tech companies and an incubator for IT and digital startups.
- **Novation City (Sousse).** A competitiveness cluster built around mechatronics, which mixes mechanics, electronics and computing.

Many tech companies are also based outside these parks, in city offices or coworking spaces. So use the parks as a starting point, not the full map.

A simple method: list 20 to 30 companies from a park or region, then check each company's careers page and LinkedIn page once a week.

## Where can I find IT internship offers online?

### Company career pages

Many companies post internships and PFE subjects only on their own website or LinkedIn page. Check them directly, especially at the start of the academic year.

### Job boards and Nextward

Job boards collect offers from many companies. On Nextward, you can filter [internships](/opportunities/internships) by type, location and remote eligibility from Tunisia, save your searches, and open the original application page in one click.

### LinkedIn

Follow the companies on your list and set job alerts for words like "stage", "PFE", "intern" and your stack. Engineers and team leads also post openings on their own profiles, so follow people as well as companies.

### Your school and its partners

Career offices, job fairs and partner companies often share PFE subjects with students first. Ask your teachers too. Many keep contact with former students who now hire interns.

### Clubs, hackathons and tech events

Student clubs give you projects, contacts and visibility. For example, the IEEE Tunisia Section has student branches in many engineering schools and universities. Hackathons and coding competitions are also a good way to meet companies: some use them to spot future interns.

## When should I start looking for an IT internship?

Start early. Many companies publish PFE subjects well before the internship begins, and good subjects go fast. For a summer internship, apply several weeks before the end of the semester. Always check each offer's deadline.

Our guides on the [PFE internship in Tunisia](/blog/pfe-internship-tunisia) and the [summer internship in Tunisia](/blog/summer-internship-tunisia) explain each type in detail.

## What skills do companies look for in IT interns?

Most teams look for solid basics more than many tools:

- One programming language you know well.
- Git: branches, commits, pull requests.
- Databases and SQL.
- How the web works: HTTP, APIs, JSON.
- Basic testing and debugging.
- Reading technical docs in English and French.

Then each track adds its own skills:

| Track | Useful skills | Project idea |
|---|---|---|
| Web | A front-end framework, a back-end framework, REST APIs | A small app with login, a database and a deployed demo |
| Mobile | One mobile framework, calling an API, app store basics | An app that reads data from a public API |
| Data | Python, SQL, data cleaning, charts, basic statistics | An analysis of a public dataset with clear conclusions |
| DevOps and cloud | Linux, Docker, CI pipelines, one cloud provider | A pipeline that tests and deploys one of your apps |
| Security | Networks, Linux, web security basics | Write-ups of practice challenges you solved |
| Embedded | C, microcontrollers, electronics basics | A small sensor project with clear documentation |

Soft skills count too. Can you explain your code? Do you ask questions when you are stuck? Can you write a clear message? Interviewers check all of this.

## How do I build a GitHub profile that helps?

Recruiters often spend only a short time on your profile. Make that time count:

1. **Pin your best repositories.** GitHub lets you pin up to six repositories and gists. Pick two or three strong ones rather than six weak ones.
2. **Add a profile README.** Create a public repository with the same name as your username, and add a README.md file. Write who you are, what you are looking for, and your main skills.
3. **Write a good README for each project:** what it does, a screenshot, how to run it, the stack, and what you learned.
4. **Show your own work.** For group projects, say clearly which parts you built.
5. **Deploy a demo** when you can, so recruiters can try the project without installing it.
6. **Keep commits clean** with short, clear messages. It shows how you would work in a team.
7. **Avoid copied tutorials.** If you followed one, change it, extend it, and say so.

## How do I make my application stand out?

- Match your CV to the offer. Put the technologies from the offer first if you really know them. Our [internship CV guide](/blog/internship-cv) shows how.
- Put your GitHub and demo links at the top of your CV.
- Write a short, specific message: one line on the company, one on the project you want to work on, one on your best related project.
- Prepare for a technical test or a live coding exercise. Practise explaining your thinking out loud. Our guide to [internship interview questions](/blog/internship-interview-questions) covers the rest of the interview.

Apply to several companies at once and keep a simple table of where you applied, when, and the answer. A steady search beats one big push.
