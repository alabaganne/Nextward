Most summer internships in Tunisia take place between June and August and often last one to two months, depending on your school's rules. Start looking in winter, apply with a one-page CV and a short message, and get your school's paperwork ready before your first day.

## What types of summer internships exist?

The names change from one school to another, but you will often see three types.

### Discovery internship (stage ouvrier or stage d'initiation)

This one usually comes at the end of the first year. You watch how a company works from the inside: production, operations, teams and rules. At ENIT, for example, the « stage ouvrier » takes place at the end of the first year, lasts at least one month, and must be validated by a defense in front of a jury.

### Technical internship (stage technicien, stage ingénieur or stage de perfectionnement)

This one usually comes after the second year. You work on a real task linked to your field. At ENIT, the engineering internship lasts at least four weeks, between the second and third year, in a company or a research lab.

### Voluntary internship

Your school does not require it, but it helps you test a field, build your CV, or meet a company that could host your PFE later.

## When should you start looking for a summer internship?

Earlier than most students think. For summer 2024, ENIT told its students that the search for internships started on 15 December. Its internships had to take place between 3 June and 30 August.

Here is a simple plan for a July internship:

| When | What to do |
|---|---|
| December–January | Read your school's rules and dates, list 20–30 companies |
| February–March | Send applications and spontaneous requests |
| April–May | Follow up, pass interviews, confirm your dates |
| May–June | Get the school's letter or agreement signed by the company |
| Last week of the internship | Collect your certificate and finish your report |

## Where can you find a summer internship in Tunisia?

- **Nextward.** Browse [summer internships](/opportunities/internships?program_type=summer), filter by field and city, save your search, and apply on the original page.
- **Your school's internship office.** Some companies send offers straight to schools, and staff know which hosts took students before.
- **Company websites and LinkedIn.** Larger companies often run a summer internship campaign with a deadline.
- **Public companies, banks and administrations.** They take many interns, but the process can be slow. In July 2025, La Presse described a student accepted by a public bank who waited weeks for news before his start date. Ask early for the start date and the person to contact.
- **Your network.** Family, teachers and older students can point you to a company. Still apply formally, with a CV and a message.
- **Abroad.** Student exchange networks such as IAESTE, which has a committee in Tunisia, offer technical internships abroad for engineering and science students.

## How do you apply with little or no experience?

1. **Pick the right type.** Check which internship your school requires this year, and for how long.
2. **Write a one-page CV.** Lead with your courses, class projects, clubs and tools. Our guide on [writing an internship CV](/blog/internship-cv) shows how to fill the page without work experience.
3. **Write a short message.** Give your school, year, dates and the type of internship, then say what you want to learn. Our [cover letter guide](/blog/internship-cover-letter) has a structure you can shorten for a summer internship.
4. **Call or visit if there is no reply.** This often works with small companies, which rarely post offers.
5. **Track your applications.** Note each company, contact, date and answer.

### What to say when you have nothing to show yet

Mention a class project, a club role, a tool you taught yourself or a short online course. For a first-year internship, recruiters mostly look for motivation and reliability. Show both: be clear, polite, and on time.

## What paperwork do you need for a summer internship?

Each school has its own process, so ask your internship office early. At ENIT, for the « stage ouvrier »:

- the company fills in and stamps a proposal form,
- the student hands it to the school,
- the school issues an assignment letter (lettre d'affectation) that the student gives to the company on day one,
- at the end, the student hands in a certificate, an internship journal and a report checked by the company, before the defense.

Common documents to ask your school about:

- **A request letter or internship agreement** (convention de stage) for the company to sign.
- **Insurance.** ENIT's form says its students are covered by the Mutuelle des accidents scolaires et universitaires during internships in Tunisia only. Ask what your school covers, especially for an internship abroad.
- **An internship certificate** (attestation de stage) at the end, with the topic, place and dates.
- **A report and a defense**, if your school requires them. Our guide to the [internship report](/blog/internship-report-pfe-defense) can help.

## How do you get the most from a summer internship?

- **Keep a journal.** Write down tasks, tools and problems every day or every week. It makes the report much easier.
- **Ask for a clear task in the first week.** If nobody gives you one, suggest a small one yourself.
- **Note results with numbers.** « Cleaned 2,000 records » or « cut a report from two days to one » reads better on a CV than « helped the team ».
- **Ask for feedback before the last week**, not on the last day.
- **Stay in touch.** A summer internship is a good way to find your future [PFE internship](/blog/pfe-internship-tunisia). La Presse notes that, for students entering their third year, summer internships are a step toward the PFE.
