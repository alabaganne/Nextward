A PFE report usually follows the same order: front pages, a general introduction, a chapter on the context, the chapters that show your work, a general conclusion, references and annexes. The defense (soutenance) is a short talk followed by questions from a jury. Every university and school has its own template, page limits and grading rules, so get your school's guide first and follow it, even where it differs from this article.

## Does every school use the same PFE template?

No. Rules change from one school to another, and sometimes from one department to another. To show how much they can differ, here is what two published Tunisian documents say:

| Rule | ISEAHM Mahdia guide (2025–2026) | ESIP Gafsa jury form (engineering) |
|---|---|---|
| Report length | 35 to 45 pages | Not stated on the form |
| Presentation | 10 minutes, then questions; whole session up to 45 minutes | Not stated on the form |
| Jury | 3 members: president, reviewer (rapporteur), supervisor | President, reviewer, school supervisor, company supervisor |
| Plagiarism | Similarity must not exceed 12% | Not stated on the form |
| Final mark | Report 9/20, defense 9/20, work with the supervisor 2/20 | 30% reviewer, 40% defense, 30% supervisors |

These are examples only. Your own school may ask for something else. Before you write a single page, find out:

- The official cover page and template.
- Page limits, font, spacing and margins.
- The language of the report and of the abstracts.
- The plagiarism check and the maximum similarity rate, if there is one.
- The deposit deadline, the number of printed copies and the digital format.
- The length of the defense and who sits on the jury.
- What your host company allows you to publish.

## What is the typical structure of a PFE report?

Most PFE reports follow this plan. Adjust it to your school's guide.

1. **Cover page:** always on your school's model.
2. **Dedication (optional) and acknowledgements.**
3. **Abstract and keywords:** some schools want them in two or three languages.
4. **Table of contents**, then lists of figures, tables and abbreviations if needed.
5. **General introduction:** context, problem, goals, method, and the plan of the report.
6. **Context chapter:** the host company, its activity, and why the project exists.
7. **Analysis chapter:** what already exists, the needs, and the gap your project fills.
8. **Work chapters:** design and build for a technical project, or method and results for a study.
9. **General conclusion:** what you achieved, the limits, and the next steps.
10. **References:** every source you used.
11. **Annexes:** long tables, extra screens, code extracts, questionnaires.

### For a technical (IT or engineering) report

The work chapters often cover: requirements, design (for example UML diagrams or architecture), the build, and the tests. Show screens and results, not only theory.

### For a business or management report

The work chapters often cover: a diagnosis of the situation, the analysis (data, interviews, benchmarks), and your recommendations with their expected impact.

## How do I write the report without rushing at the end?

1. **Keep a log from week one.** Each week, note what you did, the problems and the decisions. This becomes your report material.
2. **Agree on the plan early** with both your school supervisor and your company supervisor.
3. **Write chapters as you go.** The context chapter can be written in the first weeks.
4. **Write the introduction last.** It is easier once you know what you achieved.
5. **Give a source for every figure** you did not make, and cite every text you use. Copying without citing is plagiarism, and schools sanction it.
6. **Send drafts to your supervisor** chapter by chapter, not all at the end.
7. **Leave at least a week** for layout, spelling and the final check against your school's rules.

Ask your company early what you can include. Some data, client names or code may be confidential. You can often replace them with sample data or blur them.

## How long should my PFE defense presentation be?

Ask your school. Times vary a lot. For example, the ISEAHM guide gives 10 minutes for the talk, followed by questions. In any case, time yourself during practice and never go over.

A simple slide plan:

1. Title, your name, your school and supervisors.
2. Context and the host company, in one slide.
3. The problem and the goals.
4. Your approach or method.
5. Two to four slides on the work itself (design, build, results).
6. A short demo or video, if it fits.
7. Limits and next steps.
8. Conclusion, then thanks.

Keep slides light. The ISEAHM guide, for example, suggests large text (around size 18) and few words per slide, with one main idea each. If you plan a live demo, also record a short video in case the network or the laptop fails.

## What questions does the jury ask at a PFE defense?

You cannot guess every question. But these come up often:

- Why did you choose this solution and not another one?
- What was your own work, compared with the team or the existing code?
- How did you test it? How do you know it works?
- What are the limits of your work?
- What would you do with three more months?
- Why this technology, tool or method?
- Can you explain this term or this figure on your slide?

### How to answer well

- Listen to the full question. Take notes if there are several.
- Answer briefly, then stop. The jury will ask for more if needed.
- If several remarks are on the same topic, group them in one answer.
- If you do not know, say so calmly. Do not invent an answer.
- Stay polite, even when a remark feels harsh. Critical questions are normal.

## How do I prepare in the last two weeks?

1. **Two weeks before:** finish the report and send it to your supervisor for a last review.
2. **Ten days before:** build your slides from the report's plan.
3. **One week before:** rehearse out loud with a timer, then in front of friends or classmates. Ask them to ask you hard questions.
4. **Three days before:** rehearse once more. Prepare answers to the questions above.
5. **The day before:** save your slides in PDF on a USB key and online. Test your demo offline.
6. **On the day:** dress neatly, arrive early, and check the projector and the cable.

## What happens after the defense?

The jury deliberates and gives you a mark. It may ask for corrections. At some schools, you can only deposit the final version after the jury president approves those corrections. Check the deadline so your diploma is not delayed.

Then put your PFE to work. Add it to your CV with the results you achieved. It is often your best talking point in job interviews.
