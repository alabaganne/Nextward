Most internship interviews ask the same few kinds of questions: who you are, why you want this internship, what you have already built or done, how you work with others, and practical details like dates. Prepare a short answer with one real example for each type, and practise saying them out loud. One focused week of preparation is usually enough.

## What questions are asked in an internship interview?

Recruiters know you are a student. They do not expect years of experience. They want to see that you can learn, that you understand the role, and that you will be easy to work with.

Here are the main types of questions and what each one checks:

| Type | Example question | What the recruiter checks |
|---|---|---|
| About you | "Tell me about yourself." | Can you sum yourself up clearly? |
| Motivation | "Why this internship and this company?" | Did you read the offer and do some research? |
| Projects | "Walk me through a project you are proud of." | What did you actually do yourself? |
| Behaviour | "Tell me about a time you disagreed with a teammate." | How do you act under pressure or in a team? |
| Practical | "When can you start, and for how long?" | Do your dates and school rules fit their plan? |
| Technical | A short exercise, a case or a few questions | Do you have the basics for the role? |

You will not always get all six. But if you have an answer ready for each type, few questions will surprise you.

## How do I answer "Tell me about yourself"?

Keep it to about one minute. Use a simple order: present, past, future.

1. **Present:** what you study, your year, and your school.
2. **Past:** one or two things that link to the role, such as a project, a club or a summer internship.
3. **Future:** what you want to learn in this internship and why this role fits.

Example: "I'm in my second year of a computer science degree. Last semester I built a small booking app with two classmates, and I handled the database and the API. I enjoyed the back-end part most, so I'm looking for an internship where I can work on a real back-end team and learn how code is reviewed and deployed."

Do not repeat your whole CV. The recruiter already has it. Pick the parts that matter for this role.

## How do I answer "Why do you want this internship?"

A good answer has three parts:

- **The company:** one specific thing you learned about it, like a product, a client type or a recent project.
- **The role:** two or three tasks from the offer that you want to do.
- **You:** what you will learn, and what you can already bring.

Avoid answers like "to gain experience" on their own. Every candidate can say that. Show that you chose this offer for a reason.

## How do I talk about my projects if I have no work experience?

Use what you have: course projects, your PFA, club work, volunteering, hackathons or personal projects. These all count.

To tell the story clearly, use the STAR method:

- **Situation:** the context, in one or two sentences.
- **Task:** what you had to achieve.
- **Action:** what you did, step by step. This should be the longest part.
- **Result:** what happened, with a number if you have one.

Say "I" when you describe your own actions, not "we". The recruiter wants to know your part, even in a group project.

Example: "In a group project, our app was too slow to load the product list (situation). I was in charge of fixing it before the demo (task). I measured the slow queries, added an index and paginated the results (action). Loading time dropped a lot and the demo went well (result)."

## What behavioural questions should I prepare?

These come up often. Prepare one short story for each, or four or five stories that can cover several of them:

- A mistake you made and what you changed after.
- A conflict or disagreement in a team.
- A tight deadline and how you organised your time.
- Something you had to learn fast.
- A time you took initiative without being asked.
- Feedback you received and what you did with it.

The goal is not to look perfect. It is to show that you learn and that you can work with others.

## How do I answer tricky questions about weaknesses, pay and dates?

### "What is your main weakness?"

Pick a real but small weakness, then say what you do about it. For example: "I used to say yes to too many tasks. Now I write down my tasks with deadlines and ask for priorities when I'm unsure."

### Questions about pay

If the offer does not say whether the internship is paid, it is fine to ask. Do it politely, near the end or when the recruiter brings it up. Keep the tone neutral: "Could you tell me if the internship includes a stipend?"

### Dates and paperwork

Know your exact start and end dates before the interview. If your school needs an internship agreement (convention de stage), say so and ask who handles it on the company side. Clear answers here make you look organised.

## What should I ask the recruiter at the end?

Always have two or three questions ready. Good ones:

- "What would a typical week look like for the intern?"
- "Who would I work with day to day?"
- "What would make this internship a success for you?"
- "How do you give feedback to interns?"
- "What are the next steps after this interview?"

Avoid questions that the offer already answers. They show you did not read it.

## How do I prepare for an internship interview in one week?

1. **Day 1:** Read the offer again. Highlight the tasks and skills. Write down why you match each one.
2. **Day 2:** Research the company: website, products, recent news, and the team if you can find it.
3. **Day 3:** Write short answers for the six question types above. Use bullet points, not full scripts.
4. **Day 4:** Prepare your STAR stories and check each one has a clear result.
5. **Day 5:** Practise out loud. Record yourself or ask a friend to play the recruiter.
6. **Day 6:** Prepare for the technical part if the role has one. Review the basics listed in the offer.
7. **Day 7:** Sort out logistics. For an online interview, test your camera, microphone and connection, and find a quiet room. For an in-person interview, check the address and leave early.

Also re-read your own CV and cover letter. The recruiter may ask about any line in them. If you need to improve them first, see our guides on the [internship CV](/blog/internship-cv) and the [internship cover letter](/blog/internship-cover-letter).

## What should I do after the interview?

- Send a short thank-you email the same day or the next day. Mention one thing you discussed.
- Write down the questions you were asked. They will help you next time.
- If they gave you a date and you hear nothing after it, send one polite follow-up.
- Keep applying until you sign an offer. One good interview is not yet an internship.
